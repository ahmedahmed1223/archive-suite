Outcome

Understand the current capabilities and choose the best place to begin your task.

Before you begin

Sign in and confirm your account role because some capabilities and chapters are available only to Editors or Administrators.

Current capabilities

  • A unified media studio brings the player, a synced transcript, timeline comments and markers, version compare with clip lists, and the derivatives tree together in one screen, alongside a dedicated transcript and subtitle editor with save, lock, and version restore.
  • External review links can be shared with people who don't have an account, with their own expiry, download permission, and an optional visible watermark, and can collect one or more approval decisions before material counts as approved.
  • Settings now offers ready-made preset profiles — Archivist, Reviewer, Media editor, and Simple view — that set your home page, navigation order, and archive layout in one action, plus a home-page activity summary of recent additions and description completeness.
  • A unified work inbox brings your pending tasks, reviews, expiring rights, and notifications into one filterable list, backed by automation rules, task templates, target durations with escalation, and an approval-requests queue for sensitive bulk changes.
  • Native packages are fully supported for local operation and supported distributions, while preserving the same operational path across supported environments.
  • Choose the interface language in Settings. Full support for English is now complete, and Arabic remains fully supported as well.
  • Stronger security protections: real content-security enforcement, request rate limiting, and cross-origin controls are now active, not just displayed as settings.
  • More reliable encrypted backups: encrypted backups now stream instead of loading the whole archive into memory, so large archives back up and restore reliably.
  • The legacy-database (ODBC) bridge now works correctly against SQL Server, PostgreSQL, and MySQL, and reports real errors instead of looking empty when something fails.
  • The application now reports its actual connection health to the backend, so operators can tell the difference between "running" and "actually working".

Procedure

  1. Open Settings for any security configuration your deployment relies on (rate limits, allowed origins), or apply a preset to get started quickly.
  2. Open the chapter for your task and follow its verification steps.
  3. Review System status if an operation does not complete as expected.

Verify

Confirm that the interface behaves as expected and that any encrypted backups you rely on restore correctly.
